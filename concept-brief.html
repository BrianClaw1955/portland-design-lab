Portland Design Initiative · Pilot Concept Brief v1

Portland Design Lab

Draft v1 · 2026-09-11 · Dave (decision) / Brian (ops) / Hal (design & structure)

The idea in one sentence: A small, cross-disciplinary Portland design workshop where adult and youth cohorts go inside how design actually happens, then become participants — observing a real neighborhood, identifying a real problem, and producing a tangible prototype they present publicly.

Why now / the gap

Portland already has discipline-based festivals (Portland Design Month, ADX Fest) and a deep design-event history (Design Week Portland, Design Portland). What’s missing is an experience + methodology program: curated mixed-discipline cohorts who participate in the design process rather than attend lectures.

That gap is a modern translation of Cooper Hewitt’s historic “A City of Neighborhoods” method — observation → discussion → making → critique.

Pilot scope (a test, not the full vision)

  • 3-day compressed arc (target Tue Feb 9 – Thu Feb 11, 2027; fallback early April)
  • 2–4 cross-disciplinary teams
  • Two parallel tracks: adult cohort (~8–16) + youth citizen-designer cohort (~8–16, with mentor/guardian support)
  • Deliverable per team: a working prototype + a public presentation/critique
  • End with a community showcase

The method (original curriculum, built for Portland)

Adapted from Cooper Hewitt’s 9-step arc — but all materials written fresh, Portland-specific, legal-clean. (This is a working name; not final branding.)

  1. Understand the place
  2. Primary evidence (field packet: maps, photos, history, demographics)
  3. Go into the neighborhood
  4. Identify a real problem/opportunity
  5. Work with designers (assist, don’t dictate)
  6. Generate alternatives
  7. Build it (prototype)
  8. Present + critique
  9. Return to community/classroom

Partners (network, not competition)

Exploratory outreach only — each supplies experiences, talent, space, or audiences; nothing formalized pre-pilot. Shortlist:

AIGA Portland

Communication & graphic design

ADX Fest

Design Portland

Civic / urban design

PSU — Center for Public Interest Design

Students, education, community design

TextileX / Volumes Design Library

Textiles & craft / public design space

What success looks like (pilot metrics)

  • Cohorts recruit and show (adult + youth)
  • Each team completes a real prototype + public presentation
  • Facilitators hold the “participate, don’t just attend” arc
  • Participant + partner feedback captured
  • Ops cost known (actuals) + feasibility for a full event
  • A documented Go/No-Go recommendation for scaling

What we deliberately are NOT

  • Not another calendar-based “design festival”
  • Not a lecture-heavy conference
  • Not an attempt to own/rebrand Cooper Hewitt’s program or name
  • Not a full-scale event yet — the pilot decides what scales
