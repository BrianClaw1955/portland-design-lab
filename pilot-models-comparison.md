# Pilot Models — Operational Comparison (Brian → Hal, for Dave)

Date: 2026-09-11 · Owner: Brian (ops) · For: Hal's curriculum/program architecture review + Dave's decision on pilot structure.

## Model A — Compressed Pilot (2–3 days)
- **Format:** One concentrated push: D1 context + field observation → D2 build → D3 present/critique/showcase.
- **Participant capacity:** Small — 2–4 cross-disciplinary teams, ~8–16 adult + ~8–16 youth. Tightly carded, curated cohorts.
- **Venue needs:** ONE hands-on-capable venue (prototyping space + presentation room). A single studio, Volumes Design Library, or a PSU lab space. Low venue surface.
- **Staffing/facilitators:** Lean — 1 lead facilitator + ~1 facilitator per 6–8 participants, 2–3 support/ops staff, 1 youth-track mentor/guardian layer. ~6–9 total staff.
- **Neighborhood/field:** ONE neighborhood, in walking/transit distance of the venue. Single field packet.
- **Cost categories (order of magnitude):** venue (1×), facilitator fees, materials/prototyping kits, field materials, insurance, comm/registration, meals, contingency. **Low-mid; easiest to fund and de-risk.**
- **Partners required (minimal):** 1–2 venue/space partners + 2–3 design practitioners (AIA/ASLA/AIGA) + PSU-CPID for youth/education + possibly Design Portland for a neighborhood hook.
- **Pros:** Fast to run, low cost, low logistical surface, rapid validation of the method.
- **Cons:** Compressed time for real observation/making; less chance for distributed discovery; format may under-represent "immersion."

## Model B — Distributed Immersion (5–7 days)
- **Format:** Multi-day immersion across multiple neighborhoods/design environments: visits inside real design studios + field observation + facilitated reflection + build/workshop days + final presentation.
- **Participant capacity:** Larger — 4–8 teams, ~30–60 total across adult + youth, with staggered/rotating schedules.
- **Venue needs:** MULTIPLE venues — a hub/staging space + 2–4 partner studios + 1–2 field/neighborhood sites. Higher venue surface and coordination.
- **Staffing/facilitators:** Heavier — lead + ~1 facilitator per 6–8 per track + studio hosts at each site + ops/volunteer core + youth mentor layer. ~12–20 staff.
- **Neighborhood/field:** 2–4 neighborhoods/sites; multiple field packets; more transportation planning.
- **Cost categories (order of magnitude):** venue/hub (multi-day), site/studio access, facilitators (more days), materials, **transportation/logistics**, insurance, meals, comm, **lodging feasibility for non-local participants**, contingency. **Mid-high; more funding needed.**
- **Partners required (broad):** the org network actually activated — AIGA, ADX (makers), AIA/ASLA (studios), Design Portland (civic), PSU-CPID (youth/education), TextileX/Volumes (space), possibly Winter Light Festival (adjacency/touchpoint).
- **Pros:** True immersion; richer participant experience; showcases the "enter the design community" proposition; stronger partner activation.
- **Cons:** Significantly higher cost + logistics; more time for facilitators/partners; longer commitment for participants; harder to de-risk as a first run.

## Bottom-line comparison (for Dave)

| Factor | A: Compressed (2–3d) | B: Immersion (5–7d) |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity | ~16–32 | ~30–60 |
| Venue surface | 1 venue | hub + 2–4 sites |
| Staff | ~6–9 | ~12–20 |
| Relative cost | Low-mid | Mid-high |
| Funding ease | Easier | Harder |
| De-risk value | High (proves method) | Lower (more to go wrong first) |
| Fidelity to vision | Partial | Full |

## Recommendation
**Run Model A (compressed) as the pilot**, but design its curriculum so a Model-B scale-up is a structural extension, not a rebuild. Compressed pilot validates: the methodology (participate-don't-attend), the adult+youth parallel tracks, ONE neighborhood field method, and the minimal partner set. If it lands, B extends the same architecture across more sites/partners. This matches "pilot-first" and keeps downside risk low.
