# Model A — Program Design (3-Day Compressed Pilot)

Status: DRAFT v1 · 2026-09-11 · Locked: Model A pilot, adult + youth tracks · Brainstorming the actual program/activities.
This is the *what actually happens* — the sequence of experiences, not logistics.

## Design principles (from Hal's positioning + Cooper Hewitt method)
1. Participate, don't attend — every block ends in doing, not listening.
2. The neighborhood is the studio — real place, real problem, real material.
3. Practitioners assist, don't dictate.
4. End with tangible work + public critique, not a "winner."
5. Adults and youth share the method; the framing and depth differ.

---

## Day 1 — Understand the Place (go in)
Theme: ground the cohorts in Portland, the method, and a real neighborhood.

### Morning (town hall / orientation)
- **Welcome + why this exists** — Dave/Owner sets the vision (short, 10 min).
- **Big-picture talks (3 × 10-min lightning talks):** a historian, an architect/urbanist, a community leader — each on "how Portland's [place/design/community] works." Not lectures; they plant the questions.
- **The method, in plain words** — facilitator explains the 9-step arc using a vivid example (e.g., the High Line story: a neighborhood + an undecided future). Cohort sees the whole journey before starting.
- **Form the teams** — mixed-discipline teams of 4–6 (adult track) and 4–6 (youth track), each with an assigned practitioner-mentor.

### Midday
- **Field packet handoff** — each team gets the packet: historic maps, old photos, neighborhood history, demographics, a list of "unresolved" local questions. Primary evidence = the launchpad.
- **Lunch (intentional):** teams eat together, start annotating the packet, "what excites / concerns us about this place?"

### Afternoon — THE WOULD-BE (big "go into the neighborhood")
- **Walking tour (2–2.5 hrs):** the whole cohort, in teams, guided into ONE Portland neighborhood. Warm-up exercise: each person photographs/notes ONE thing that works and ONE thing that's broken/odd/undecided. Shared afterward, 5 min per team.
- **Immersion prompt (end of day / homework):** "By tomorrow noon, each team picks ONE unresolved problem or opportunity in this neighborhood that could plausibly be addressed in a day of making." Practitioner-mentors give honest feasibility flags.

### Evening (optional, low-key)
- **Open studio / show-and-tell:** teams pin up their first photos + early problem candidates. Casual; momentum-building, not graded.

---

## Day 2 — Make It (the build)
Theme: the working day — observation resolves into a real problem, then gets built.

### Morning
- **Problem lock (1 hr):** each team commits to ONE problem/opportunity. Facilitators enforce scope — "specific enough to address in a day." Poster it.
- **Practice session / warm-up build (45 min):** a fast group exercise — e.g., "prototype a better bus stop in 20 minutes with cardboard + tape," to get hands moving and de-intimidate making.
- **Field re-check (30–45 min):** teams revisit the neighborhood or interrogate the packet for the specific site of their problem.

### Midday/Lunch
- **Designer drop-ins:** 2–3 practitioners rotate between teams ("office hours") — architects, product/landscape/graphic designers. Advise, don't dictate.

### Afternoon — THE BUILD (main event)
- **Prototype/build block (3–4 hrs):** teams physically build — cardboard, clay, wire, found materials, photographs, models, mock-ups, posters. Practitioner-mentors embedded for technique only. This is the heart: North-scravery, taper, tools, tape.
- **Mid-build check-in (20 min):** each team does a 2-minute "here's what we're making and why" pause; table critique (kind, specific).

### Late afternoon
- **Pre-presentation shaping (1 hr):** teams finish the artifact + prep a 5-minute public story: the problem, the idea, the build, the ask.

---

## Day 3 — Present & Return (visible)
Theme: give the work back to the community + reflect.

### Morning
- **Final polish (1.5 hrs):** teams complete the prototype + rehearsal for the public talk. Practitioner clean-up.
- **Public showcase (2–2.5 hrs):** open to community, neighborhood residents, partners, press-adjacent. Each team presents 5 min + field questions. Then a gallery walk — visitors browse all prototypes at tables. Ends with a group photo + brief reflection.
- **Critique that teaches, not grades:** facilitators model judging criteria — clarity of problem, boldness of idea, quality of making, worthiness of the response — but NO winner. Critique serves learning.

### Afternoon — RETURN IT
- **"So what / now what" reflection (45 min):** adult + youth each debrief: what changed in how you see design? how might this return to the community/classroom (their school, their org)?
- **Next-step cards:** everyone (participant + partner) writes one concrete follow-through — a partnership, a project, a teaching idea.
- **Close + handoff:** owner thanks + names the path to a full (immersion) version.

## Cross-cutting activity notes
- **Adult vs youth depth:** same method; adults get more policy/feasibility pressure, youth get more play + creativity + a school-return mechanism (teacher/mentor layer).
- **Materials as pedagogy:** keep prototyping materials raw and accessible (cardboard, tape, clay, wire, model-making) — the method lives in the making, not expensive tech.
- **Documentation:** 1–2 capture crew (photos + short video) so a 3-min highlight reel + photo deck exist for partners/funders.

## Open questions for Hal (+ I'll recommend)
1. One neighborhood for the whole pilot, or let each team pick different sites within one district? (Rec: one neighborhood, multiple sites — simpler ops, still distinct problems.)
2. Length of the D3 public showcase — real-public or invited? (Rec: invited + community, not a wide open gate — controllable, still meaningful.)
3. Should youth attend all 3 days, or is a weekend-staggered youth track more realistic? (Rec: co-located all 3 days if guardians/parents can commit; otherwise move youth to Sat-only.)
4. Prototype artifact range — allow purely conceptual/posters, or require a physical 3D build? (Rec: a spectrum; at least one physical artifact per team.)
