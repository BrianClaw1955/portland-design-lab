# Portland Design Lab — One-Page Concept Brief (Pilot)

Status: DRAFT v1 · 2026-09-11 · Dave (decision) / Brian (ops) / Hal (design/structure)
Purpose: lock the pilot concept for review; basis for partner outreach + funding.

## 1. The idea in one sentence
A small, cross-disciplinary Portland design workshop where adult and youth
cohorts go inside how design actually happens, then become participants —
observing a real neighborhood, identifying a real problem, and producing a
tangible prototype they present publicly.

## 2. Why now / the gap
Portland already has discipline-based festivals (Portland Design Month, ADX
Fest) and a deep design-event history (Design Week Portland, Design Portland).
What's missing is an *experience + methodology* program: curated mixed
-discipline cohorts who participate in the design process rather than attend
lectures. That gap is a modern translation of Cooper Hewitt's historic
"A City of Neighborhoods" method (observation → discussion → making → critique).

## 3. Pilot scope (this is a test, not the full vision)
- 3-day compressed arc (target Feb 9–11, 2027)
- 2–4 cross-disciplinary teams
- Two parallel tracks: **adult** cohort (~8–16) + **youth** citizen-designer
  cohort (~8–16, with mentor/guardian support)
- Deliverables per team: a working prototype + a public presentation/critique
- End with a community showcase

## 4. The method (original curriculum, built for Portland)
Adapted from Cooper Hewitt's 9-step arc — but all materials written fresh,
Portland-specific, legal-clean:
1. Understand the place → 2. Primary evidence (field packet) → 3. Go into the
neighborhood → 4. Identify a real problem/opportunity → 5. Work with
designers (assist, not dictate) → 6. Generate alternatives → 7. Build it
(prototype) → 8. Present + critique → 9. Return to community/classroom.

## 5. Partners (network, not competition)
Exploratory outreach — each supplies experiences, talent, space, or audiences;
nothing formalized pre-pilot. Shortlist: AIGA, ADX, AIA, ASLA, Design Portland,
PSU Center for Public Interest Design, TextileX, Volumes Design Library.

## 6. What success looks like (pilot metrics)
- Cohorts recruit and show (adult + youth)
- Each team completes a real prototype + public presentation
- Facilitators hold the "participate, don't just attend" arc
- Participant + partner feedback captured
- Ops cost known (actuals) + feasibility for a full event
- A documented Go/No-Go recommendation for scaling

## 7. What we deliberately are NOT
- Not another calendar-based "design festival"
- Not a lecture-heavy conference
- Not an attempt to own/rebrand Cooper Hewitt's program or name
- Not a full-scale event yet — the pilot decides what scales
